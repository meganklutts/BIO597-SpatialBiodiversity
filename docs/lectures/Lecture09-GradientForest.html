Encoding genotype data into a compact format

Count the alternate copies

Two chromosome copies give a dosage of 0, 1, or 2.

Timing: 1 minute. A SNP is a single nucleotide polymorphism. The slashes separate the two chromosome copies; the same dosage mapping works for phased calls using a vertical bar. The pyGF reader handles diploid biallelic SNP GT fields and treats missing or partial calls as NaN. Do not encode a missing genotype as zero: zero is an observed homozygous reference genotype. Source: pyGF README, https://github.com/isaacovercast/pygf. The allele pairs and sample symbols are schematic. The color key is repeated on subsequent SNP diagrams. Alternate does not mean beneficial or derived.
Thinking about filtering/QC

Filter for missingness, sufficiently variable sites, LD, and take a subset so it runs faster.

Filtering chooses which genetic variation enters the analysis.

Timing: 1.5 minutes. Call rate and MAF are computed among selected samples. For diploid calls, alternate frequency is sum of called dosages divided by twice the called sample count; MAF is the smaller of that frequency and its complement. One_per_contig takes the first eligible SNP within each RAD contig before sampling across eligible contigs; this is not formal LD pruning and would be inappropriate as one-per-chromosome filtering. Changing the filters or subset can change the biological summary. Source: course Lab09 and pyGF README. The call-rate cartoon shows 8 of 10 samples, not the real 36-sample dataset. The minor allele panel shows 1 of 20 called allele copies, exactly 5%. The final dot array symbolizes a subset and is not an exact count of 256. These are thresholds, not additional filtering results.
The gradient forest model

One random forest for each SNP

Each forest sees the same environments and learns one SNP’s dosage pattern.

Timing: 1 minute. Recall that each tree partitions climate space and predicts a mean response within a leaf. Trees receive bootstrap resamples of the observed rows for their SNP. Missing y values are excluded per SNP rather than imputed. This is a collection of separate single-response forests, whose useful information will be synthesized. Source: pyGF README Method section. Walk through one individual, such as A, across all three panels: its position never changes, while its dosage may change by locus. Then compare SNP A, which mainly tracks temperature, with SNP B, which mainly tracks rainfall, and SNP C, which has a different pattern. All twelve samples, coordinates and dosages are invented; this is a two-predictor cartoon of the 19-predictor lab workflow. Each tree glyph symbolizes a tree within the SNP-specific ensemble, not an additional response. Missing calls are omitted per SNP in the real fit.
"Out of bag" prediction error

Evaluating ability to predict genotype of holdout set of samples

Average predictions from trees that never learned from individual A.

Timing: 1.5 minutes. OOB is internal resampling, not a separately collected dataset. Each SNP can have different missing calls and hence a different set of observed rows. Other trees in the full forest can contain individual A, but its OOB prediction uses only trees that did not. Source: scikit-learn OOB example, https://scikit-learn.org/stable/auto_examples/ensemble/plot_ensemble_oob.html. Connect to Lab08: this does not enforce geographic separation. This cartoon shows only three trees for clarity; the actual forest uses 100. Bootstrap rows contain repeated IDs, illustrating sampling with replacement. The two eligible invented predictions average to 1.0, matching A’s observed dosage here. Other individuals contribute to A’s prediction, including spatial neighbors.
Evaluating model fit

Aggregating OOB prediction error across all Trees/SNPs

R² = 0 matches the mean-only benchmark; positive scores improve on it.

Timing: 1 minute. The exact formula is 1 minus sum((y - yhat_OOB)^2) divided by sum((y - mean(y))^2), over rows with OOB predictions. The reference mean is calculated over the scored observations. Constant responses are skipped; sparse responses and inadequate OOB coverage are also diagnostic concerns. Positive R² is a predictive screening rule, not a significance test for adaptation. Source: pyGF forest.py and https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.metrics.r2_score.html. In all three panels the six observed dosages are 0, 0, 1, 1, 2, 2, whose mean is 1 and total squared variation is 4. Predictions equal to the observations have SSE 0 and R² 1. Constant-one predictions have SSE 4 and R² 0. Reversed predictions have SSE 16 and R² −3. These scores are exact for the toy points, not additional results from pyGF.

Timing: 1 minute. Inspect both excluded and retained SNPs. A weakly predicted SNP can still be biologically meaningful; it simply does not supply positive modeled skill to this turnover summary. Conversely, a retained SNP is not automatically an adaptive locus. The histogram shows real fitted results; exact counts and score range are recorded in assets/Lecture09-example-results.json and assets/Lecture09-snp-scores.csv.
Evaluating climate variable importance

Shuffle climate; see what breaks

If prediction error grows, the fitted forest relied on that variable.

Timing: 1 minute. The forest is not refitted after shuffling. We break part of its predictive information at evaluation time. pyGF stores raw OOB MSE increases in permutation_importances_. This differs from the impurity decrease importance we saw in a simple random forest: both appear in the GF algorithm, but serve different roles. Source: pyGF README Method section; general principle at https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/permutation_importance.html. The original temperatures are 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15; observed dosages are 0, 0, 1, 1, 2, 2. The fixed toy rule returns 0 below 8, 1 from 8 to below 12, and 2 from 12 onward. The shuffled temperatures yield squared errors totaling 8 across 6 observations, MSE 1.33. Individual labels and dosage colors do not move vertically. This is an exact marginal-shuffle illustration, not a fitted SNP result; the next slide introduces the lab’s approximate conditional permutation.
Combining the forests

Share useful signal among the predictors

Give predictors a share of each SNP’s OOB prediction accuracy, then average over contributing SNPs.

Timing: 1.5 minutes. Normalize positive permutation MSE increases to sum to one for each retained response, then multiply by its positive OOB R². The allocations sum to that response score. Importance_ reports their mean across contributing responses, not a mean across all attempted SNPs and not a fraction summing to one. Across predictors, endpoints sum to mean retained OOB R². This normalization rule is specific to the implementation used in the lab. Source: pyGF README Method steps 3–5. The stack lengths are invented allocations. Temperature is teal and rainfall is coral; these are predictor categories, distinct from the grayscale dosage key. The lower bars illustrate additional retained SNPs and do not report real pyGF loci.

From trees to gradients

From useful splits to a turnover curve

A cluster of informative splits becomes a steep part of the cumulative curve.

Schematic shapes. Smoothing and density correction account for where samples fall along the gradient.

Timing: 1.5 minutes. Distinguish two uses of importance: OOB permutation importance allocates predictive skill among predictors, while decreases in tree squared-error impurity distribute that allocation along each predictor. With the default density_correction=True, the smoothed split mass is divided by the smoothed observed predictor density for each response, with a floor on the denominator. This corrects uneven environmental coverage in curve construction; it does not remove observation bias, population structure, or spatial dependence. Gaussian smoothing and conditional permutations differ from R gradientForest. Sources: pyGF README Method; general synthesis principle in Ellis et al. 2012. The curves are conceptual rather than numeric transformations of the displayed rug. Coral dashed lines track the same interval through the diagram. Useful split mass is smoothed, corrected for sampling density, integrated, and scaled to allocated importance before averaging across contributing SNPs. Keep the detailed normalization in narration; use the image to explain the transition from a local peak to a cumulative rise.
The fitted example

Read the location and magnitude of turnover

Steep segment: faster modeled turnover. Endpoint: total predictor importance. X axes retain each raster’s units.

Timing: 1.5 minutes. Choose one visible steep segment and ask students to describe its environmental interval. All panels share a y limit to aid magnitude comparison. A new environmental coordinate system

Stretch climate space where turnover is fastest

The same raw climate difference can become a small or large turnover distance.

Illustration. In pyGF, transform() and predict() return these turnover coordinates.

Timing: 1 minute. Each fitted monotone curve transforms its predictor independently. Differences are larger when two raw values straddle a steep portion of that curve. The resulting coordinate differences summarize model-weighted environmental differences associated with genetic variation; they are not a direct estimate of pairwise sequence divergence. Keep the distinction from scikit-learn forest.predict, which would return dosage predictions. Source: pyGF README Python API. The toy raw intervals A–B and C–D are both 0.2 units wide. The first becomes 0.05 turnover units and the second becomes 0.70. Their distances follow interpolation through the displayed piecewise linear curve. This is one environmental coordinate; the real transformation applies each predictor’s own curve and retains one column per predictor.

Summarizing nineteen turnover coordinates

PCA gives the map and biplot a shared color key

PC1/2 locate biplot points. PC1/2/3 supply RGB colors. Similar colors indicate similar displayed coordinates.

Timing: 2 minutes. PCA rotates the centered GF-transformed coordinates toward axes capturing their largest variance. In this call the PCA basis is the complete raster cells in the buffered, downsampled region, not just sampled individuals. The first three scores become RGB channels, scaled using regional 2nd–98th percentiles; the scatter displays PC1 and PC2. pyGF does not standardize columns to unit variance, preserving the GF weighting. Similar colors are a compressed model summary, not measured genetic identity or a calibrated distance. Additional PCs and clipping can hide differences. Source: pyGF README Spatial biplots.
Reading the biplot

Read the biplot as an environmental compass

Follow an arrow toward increasing transformed values; compare its contribution to PC1/2.

Schematic PCA scores and covariance arrows. Nearby points share similar scores on the displayed axes.

Timing: 1 minute. pyGF computes components transposed times the square root of explained variance, then applies a common arrow display multiplier. Only the largest n_vectors are labeled. A variable can carry information outside PC1/2, so short arrows need not mean unimportant in the full transformed space. Return to the previous plot and ask which way to move to increase one labeled predictor. PCA is unsupervised here, but its input transformation was learned from SNPs. Source: pyGF README Spatial biplots. The ordination, colors and arrows are invented to enlarge the reading task. Arrow length concerns the displayed PC1/2 pattern, not causal effect or necessarily overall importance. The full dimensional turnover space can contain differences that this two-dimensional view hides.

Biological interpretation

The same pattern can have different explanations

A climate–genotype association gives us a hypothesis to test.

Conceptual alternatives. Fitness measurements and population-history evidence help distinguish them.

Timing: 1.5 minutes. This is inferential reasoning, not additional evidence collected in the lab. Spatially structured ancestry can align with spatially structured climate without climate causing the genetic pattern. This is especially relevant for only 36 individuals and 19 related predictors. For a spatial prediction claim, refit and evaluate SNP responses in geographic holdouts, with filtering and tuning confined to training information. Genotype prediction error can be evaluated there; the turnover map itself has no directly observed ground-truth color. OOB internal prediction and spatial transfer are different questions. Source for the implementation limits: pyGF README Data and interpretation. Both genotype strips are intentionally identical. The left proposes climate affecting genotype patterns through selection. The right proposes geography relating to climate and ancestry, with ancestry explaining genotype patterns. These are simplified candidate causal structures, not inferred causal graphs. Spatial sampling and correlated environmental variables remain additional alternatives; discuss them using the previous notes.
